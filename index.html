About

I'm Eduardo Carranza, a senior graphic designer in Northern California with thirteen years of experience across brand identity, print and digital. Most of my strongest work is identity — logos, marks, and the guideline documents that keep them intact long after they've left the studio.

I spent thirteen years at PreZero and Roplast, starting as a graphic artist and moving up through media manager to a senior specialist role — so this work is in-house, not freelance. I owned pieces end to end: packaging artwork, trade-show environments, prepress, vendor and order management, and the site and social work that carries the same brand voice across print and screen. A degree in Multimedia Design from DeVry University gave me the production side; the trade-show floors taught me the rest.

Alongside that I take on outside commissions, which is where the conference collateral for Grubbets sits. Pieces are marked In-house or Client so the distinction is never ambiguous.

That background means designing against hard, unglamorous constraints: a 48×36 poster that has to be readable from across a convention hall, a back label that has to satisfy a compliance panel, a bag that has to survive a warehouse. Those constraints travel with me — whatever the sector.

Identity work in particular rewards restraint. A mark has to hold at a millimetre on a bottle cap and at three metres on a wall, in one colour or two, without anyone redrawing it. I care most about the unglamorous part: the clear space, the minimum size, the do-not list. That's what makes a guideline worth more than the logo file.
